THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE — break it and everything must be redone.
Each scene has TWO images. Use them as the FIRST FRAME and LAST FRAME of one video.
  Start image = first frame (the clean scene, no infographics)
  End image   = last frame  (the same scene with the infographics added)
Never animate a single image. The video must travel between the two.
Pick the generation mode that accepts both a first frame and a last frame.
Never pair images from different scenes. Only same-numbered scenes go together.
Do not add or drop scenes. Make exactly one video per scene in the list. Do not stop after one — finish them all.

Each video keeps the start image's subject, structure, layout, background, lighting, colors and design,
while the end image's infographic elements are built up step by step, so the final frame matches the end image.
No plain crossfade. No simple zoom on a still image.
The character never changes colour, markings or accessories during the video. It must stay exactly this:
{{character}}

WHAT MATTERS MOST
1. A camera that genuinely moves through 3D space.
2. Real motion of the subject and environment, with infographic motion graphics that explain that motion.
Every scene = moving 3D scene + camera travelling through real space + infographics explaining the motion.

1) CAMERA IN 3D SPACE
Not 2D zoom or pan — the camera's position actually moves through depth. Choose per scene:
fast push from wide to the key subject / descend or rise / enter from outside into an interior or cutaway /
forward dive toward a part / track along a path / orbit then approach / strong foreground-background parallax /
cinematic fly-through / wide-to-detail or detail-to-wide.
Tie every camera move to the information flow. Never write vague "dynamic camera" — state start position,
direction, target, entry point and final position.
Clay stop-motion feel: slightly stepped motion is welcome, like real frame-by-frame animation.

2) CUTAWAYS AND INTERIORS
When the idea is about something inside (a body, a throat, a mechanism), do not just float graphics over the outside.
Show the whole → approach → the surface turns translucent or opens → the inside is revealed → move in close →
the motion starts → graphics follow that motion → end matching the end image.
The opening is a real spatial change in 3D, not a fade.

3) THE SCENE ITSELF MOVES
Camera motion alone is not enough. Things in the start image move naturally:
the cat breathes, blinks, twitches its ears and whiskers, shifts its paws; fabric, light, dust and air move.
Infographics follow and explain that real motion.
Never create new characters, buildings, or key objects, and never delete existing ones.

4) INFOGRAPHIC MOTION
Big and sharp enough to read instantly on a phone. High-saturation, high-contrast accent colors;
different information gets different colors.
Use: thick arrows / rings, circles, arcs / glowing outlines / boundaries / translucent highlight zones /
connecting and path lines / light trails / scan effects / energy lines / pulses / callout lines / ripples.
Every graphic explains the scene — never decoration.
Build them in order, not all at once:
highlight the target → draw arrows, rings, paths → extend and connect → match the end image's finished state.
Prefer lines that actually draw, rings that expand, paths that advance, zones that light up — over fades.

5) NO TEXT
No readable text at any moment. No titles, labels, numbers, units or letters — not even for a single frame.
The end image has no text, so neither does the video.

6) FAST ZOOM — use sparingly
Only on the scenes marked "Fast zoom: yes". Short, punchy snap-zoom toward or away from the key subject,
with graphics firing at the same moment. Avoid heavy shake. Never on two neighbouring scenes in the same way.

7) STRUCTURE OF EACH 8-SECOND CLIP
Stay in ONE continuous 3D space with 2–3 connected camera beats:
  early: wide view that establishes the situation
  mid:   approach the key subject or enter the interior
  late:  the clearest view of the principle and the finished infographic
No hard cuts between beats — one continuous camera move.
Only part of each clip is used in the final edit (usually the later part), so the LAST beat must work on its own.

8) FLOW BETWEEN SCENES
Clips are joined into one video, so design them to flow:
the last camera position of one scene leads into the first of the next.
Vary the link: continue into the next space / scale out or in / hand over a visual element (a path, a line of light) /
keep the same camera direction.
Match light direction, colour temperature, haze and time of day across the cut.
Never dip to black or flash to white between scenes.
Exceptions: open the first scene wide; close the last scene by pulling back.

SOUND
Only sound effects that would really happen in the scene.
No speech, dialogue, narration or human voice — narration is added later.
No background music, no instruments.
